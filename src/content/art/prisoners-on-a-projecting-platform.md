Try tracing a path from one side of this etching to the other and see how quickly you lose your bearings. An enormous masonry arch frames the foreground, opening onto another arch behind it, and then another. High overhead, a wooden bridge lined with tiny silhouettes cuts across the vault, while a second balustraded gallery curves along the lower right. Between them hang taut diagonal ropes and a heavy lantern suspended over empty air.

On the left, a cluster of figures sits bound and hunched on a jutting timber-and-stone platform, pushed up close against the viewer while the rest of the room recedes into dizzying vertical scale. What makes Piranesi's *Carceri* series feel unsettling isn't dungeon horror—there are no neat rows of cells or obvious locks. It's the architecture itself. The staircases, piers, and catwalks look structurally plausible piece by piece, yet when you zoom out, the whole space behaves like a loop you can't map from the inside.

If you've ever stared at a sprawling legacy network or an over-nested permission graph where every doorway opens onto three more hallways, the feeling is oddly familiar. Like [The Strangling](/art/the-strangling), the frame leaves almost no quiet margin to step outside of; the onlookers on the bridges and balconies become part of the stonework. The brightest passage—the pale burst of light behind the projecting platform—doesn't mark an exit so much as prove how deep the ceiling rises above it.
